A client's visit to Hawaii Nails & Lashes for a routine french dip set describes a service that ran well past its normal length, did not match what she asked for, and ended in a way she found unsettling enough to want other clients to be aware of it. Her account is worth reviewing closely, both for the service issues and for what happened after the manicure was finished.

## A Color That Didn't Match the Request

The client says she asked for pink tips, but the technician insisted the tips were already pink when, in the client's view, they were white, and she was not offered the chance to pick a different color once the disagreement came up. She adds that the technical work itself looked great. A simple way to avoid this kind of disagreement is to confirm the exact shade against a swatch or sample before applying it, and if a client says the result does not match what she asked for, offering to show or swap the color in the moment rather than insisting the original choice was correct.

## A Service That Took Twice as Long

She also notes that a service which normally takes about 45 minutes stretched to an hour and thirty minutes. Even strong technical work can leave a client dissatisfied if the appointment runs significantly longer than expected without any explanation for the delay. When a service is going to take meaningfully longer than usual, letting the client know partway through, along with a brief reason, helps her feel informed rather than simply left waiting.

## Being Asked to Post a Review That Wasn't Hers to Post

The most serious part of the client's account involves what happened after the service. She says staff asked to take photos of her hands, which she agreed to, and had her text the photos to herself from their phone. She then describes being asked whether she had received the photos, after which her own phone was taken from her hand, a review page was opened, and she was told to write a review using the photos. When she said she would do it later and took her phone back, she says it was taken from her again and a five-star review with the photo was posted from her own phone without her wording or consent. She says she deleted it once she got to her car, and that she was alone in the salon at the time, which she found frightening. Posting a review on a client's behalf, without her explicit agreement to the content and timing, is not a legitimate way to gather feedback, and taking a client's phone to do so crosses a clear line regardless of intent. Reviews should always be left entirely at the client's discretion, written in her own words, on her own timeline, using her own device without interference.

## Editorial Perspective

A few lessons stand out from this visit. First, when a client says a color does not match what she requested, the response should be to check and adjust, not to insist the original result was correct. Second, when a service runs significantly over its normal time, a brief update to the client helps prevent frustration from building unnecessarily. Third, and most importantly, review requests should never involve taking a client's phone or posting on her behalf. Any request for a review needs to be a simple, optional ask that the client can decline or complete entirely on her own terms, since anything more coercive risks a client's trust and safety and can undermine the credibility of the salon's reviews overall.
