Some visits raise a single issue, and some touch on several at once. This report on Midwest Nail and Spa falls into the second group: a mother and daughter described a visit where the service, the comfort, and the cleaning of tools all fell short. The account is the client's own, but each concern points to a standard that keeps customers safe and coming back, which is why it is worth walking through calmly.

## The Upgraded Package That Wasn't

The visit started with a clear request. The family asked for the upgraded package, yet by their account they received a standard mani and pedi instead. The extras they had paid for, including the pressure work and callus treatment on the pedicure, were not really delivered. When a client selects and pays for a specific package, matching the service to what was purchased is the baseline, and if something can't be provided that day, it is far better to say so and adjust the price than to quietly hand over a lesser service.

## Comfort, Consent, and a Careful Touch

The experience also felt disorganized and uncomfortable. The client described being moved between technicians three times, which made the visit feel hectic, and a pairing that left her mother uneasy, partly because of a strong smell of cigarette smoke that lingered through the service. Personal presentation, including keeping the workspace and technician free of strong odors, is part of a comfortable visit.

The bigger concern was consent and care. The client said she asked the technician not to work her cuticle area, but felt the grinding continued past what she was comfortable with even after she repeated the request, and that two of her fingers were nicked by the tool. She also felt the powder dip was done too quickly to look its best. A clear "please don't do that" should stop the moment it is said, and slowing down around delicate skin protects both the client and the salon.

## Clean Tools Between Every Client

The most important concern was hygiene. The client believed tools were being shared between guests without cleaning in between, and said the same tools used on her and her mother were carried to another table for another client. When she raised it, she felt the request to sanitize was not addressed. She also reported developing an irritation under the skin a few days later. These are the client's own recollections, but they point to the one standard a salon can never relax: every tool disinfected or replaced between clients, every time, with no exceptions.

## Editorial Perspective

The encouraging part is that each of these is a clear, fixable standard rather than a matter of talent. First, deliver exactly what the client paid for, and speak up honestly if a package can't be completed that day. Second, keep the visit calm and comfortable: limit technician swaps, keep workstations and staff free of strong smells, and treat a client's "please stop" as an instruction to follow at once, working gently around cuticles and skin to avoid any nicks. Third, and above all, protect hygiene without compromise. Tools should be disinfected or swapped between every guest, sanitation should be visible and reassuring, and any client who asks about cleaning deserves a calm, welcoming answer, never to feel brushed off. A mother and daughter came in for a relaxing day together, and with the package honored, a gentler touch, and clean tools every time, that is exactly what this visit could have been.

*This account reflects the customer's own description of her experience, including the reported injury and irritation, and is shared here for training and educational purposes rather than as a verified finding.*
