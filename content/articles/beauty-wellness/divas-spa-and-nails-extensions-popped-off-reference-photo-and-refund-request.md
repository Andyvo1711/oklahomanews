A client who has been getting her nails done for years knows what a good service feels like, which is why her disappointment deserves attention. A customer of **Diva's Spa & Nails** says she has been having her nails done for more than five years and had never had such a difficult experience. Her account touches three moments every salon handles: confirming the design, adjusting mid-service, and responding when something goes wrong afterward.

## A Client Who Came Prepared

The customer says she showed a reference photo of the design she wanted before the service began. That is exactly how a client communicates clearly, and it gives the team a concrete goal. She says she was told the design could be done.

According to her, after the extensions and powder were applied, the team changed their minds and said the design could not be done. She also says she was told it was too late for her to go somewhere else.

This is a useful training moment. Before any product goes on, the technician should look at the photo, confirm what is possible, and say so honestly. If a design is outside the team's comfort zone, the best time to say so is at the start, so that the client can choose another option or another salon. Changing course midway leaves a client with fewer choices and more frustration.

## When Nails Lifted Within Hours

The customer reports that less than twelve hours after she left the salon, three of her nails popped off. She describes this as the most disappointing part of the visit. Lifting can have many causes, and a single review cannot tell us which applied here. The point for salons is how to respond.

Offer a free repair or reapplication window, and tell clients about it before they leave. Give simple aftercare tips, and ask whether anything felt loose at the end of the appointment. When a client calls with a problem, listen first, ask for details, and look for a fair solution, such as a quick fix visit.

## Refunds, Tips, and a Request to Fix Something

The customer says she called to ask for a refund and was told no, and that her tip would not be refunded either. She also says that when she asked the technician to fix something during the service, she was told no, and that she found the tone dismissive.

Policies on refunds differ among salons, and it is reasonable for a business to have one. What clients remember is the way it is explained. A clear, kind explanation, along with a repair offer or a credit, often keeps the relationship intact even when a refund is not possible. A request to fix a detail during the appointment should almost always be met with a calm yes, or a clear explanation of what can be done instead.

## Editorial Perspective

This review shows how quickly a long relationship can be affected by a few moments of uncertainty.

First, confirm the design from the reference photo before applying any product, and be honest about what is possible. Second, publish and explain a fair repair policy, so a client whose nails lift has an easy path to a fix. Third, respond to every request, whether a correction during the service or a call afterward, with patience and a clear, courteous explanation.

The customer described her $75 visit as not worth the cost and said she would not return. A salon that listens early and repairs generously can often keep a client who has been loyal for years.
