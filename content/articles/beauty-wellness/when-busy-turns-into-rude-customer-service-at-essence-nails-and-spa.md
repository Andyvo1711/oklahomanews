A busy day is a good problem to have, but it is also the moment a salon's customer service is tested the most. This report on Essence Nails & Spa describes a visit where, by the client's account, the pressure of a full shop turned into treatment that felt rude, and that was enough to send a paying customer looking elsewhere.

## Busy Is Understood, Rude Is Not

The client was clear that she understood the salon was busy. What she could not accept was feeling that the staff were short and unfriendly with her because of it. As she put it, she spends a good deal on her nails and did not feel that came with the courtesy she expected. A rush of clients can strain anyone, but it does not change the one thing every customer is looking for, which is to feel welcome and respected while they are in the chair.

## The Cost of a Lost Regular

The most telling part of the review is what it signals for the business. This was not someone unwilling to pay; she described herself as a regular spender on her nails who was ready to keep coming back. A short or dismissive interaction on a hectic day was all it took for her to decide to take her money somewhere else and not return. One rushed moment can quietly cost a salon a loyal, high-value client, and often several more through the word she shares afterward.

## Editorial Perspective

The encouraging part is that this is one of the most fixable issues a salon can face, because it costs nothing but attention. The key is having a plan for busy days, so that being full never turns into being curt. A warm greeting at the door, a quick and honest wait time, and a simple "thank you for your patience" go a long way when the shop is slammed. It also helps to make sure no client feels rushed or brushed off, even when the schedule is tight, because a calm and friendly tone under pressure is exactly what separates a salon people return to from one they leave. Training the whole team to protect that courtesy on the busiest days, and checking in with waiting clients rather than letting them feel forgotten, is what keeps a good regular instead of sending her to a competitor. Handled with a little warmth, this visit could have ended with a happy customer rather than a goodbye.
