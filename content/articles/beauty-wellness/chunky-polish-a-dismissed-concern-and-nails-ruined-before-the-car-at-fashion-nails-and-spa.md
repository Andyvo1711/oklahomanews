The finish of a pedicure is what a client walks out with, so smooth polish and proper drying are not small details, they are the whole result. This report on Fashion Nails & Spa describes a visit where the polish went on poorly, the client's concern was waved away, and the nails were not dry enough to survive the walk to her car. Each issue is easy to prevent, and together they explain a very disappointing result.

## Chunky Polish and a Concern Brushed Off

The client said her base polish was thick and unsmooth, which made every layer applied over it thick and uneven too. When she brought it to her technician's attention, she felt it was brushed off, with the technician continuing and simply saying it had to dry first. Polish that goes on chunky usually means the bottle is old or thick, or the coats are being applied too heavily, and the fix is to use fresh, smooth polish in thin, even layers. Just as important, when a client points out a problem mid-service, that is the moment to pause and correct it, not to keep going. A smooth base is the foundation of the whole finish, and getting it right early prevents every layer after it from suffering.

## No Drying, and Ruined Nails on the Way Out

The drying step was where it fell apart. The client said she was never offered a dryer to sit under and was instead told the nails were dry now and it was okay, when they were not. She ruined two nails before she even reached her car, and another once she got there. Polish needs adequate time to set, whether under a fan, a dryer, or with enough patience before a client leaves, and telling someone her nails are dry when they are still soft almost guarantees they will smudge. Confirming the nails are truly set, and offering a dryer, is a simple step that protects all the work that came before it.

## Editorial Perspective

The encouraging part is that all of this is basic and fixable. First, use smooth, fresh polish applied in thin, even coats, so the base and every layer over it stay smooth. Second, listen when a client flags a concern during the service and correct it right away rather than brushing it off. Third, never rush the drying: offer a dryer or fan, give the polish real time to set, and make sure the nails are genuinely dry before the client stands up, so they are not ruined on the way to the car. With smooth application, a listening ear, and proper drying, this client would have left with a finish that lasted, instead of one that smudged before she reached her car.
