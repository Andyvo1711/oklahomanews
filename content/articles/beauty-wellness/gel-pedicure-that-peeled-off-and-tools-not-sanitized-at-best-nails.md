A warm, friendly team is a real asset, and this client made a point of it, which is what makes the rest of her report on Best Nails worth taking seriously. Two issues stood out, one about how long the work lasted and one about hygiene, and while the first is a matter of technique, the second is the single standard a salon can never relax. Both are fixable, and the friendly service gives the salon a strong foundation to build on.

## Gel That Peeled Off Within Days

The client's gel pedicure did not last. She said one toe peeled off completely on its own the very next day, another the following day, and eight had come off within three days. Gel that lifts and peels this quickly points almost entirely to adhesion, meaning the prep and curing. Thorough prep, gently buffing and dehydrating the nail, careful application, and full curing under the lamp are what let gel bond and stay put for weeks rather than days. When nearly a whole set peels within seventy-two hours, it is a clear sign to revisit the process step by step, because the product is capable of lasting far longer when applied correctly.

## Tools Not Sanitized Between Clients

The more serious concern was hygiene. The client said she noticed the same tools being used on every customer without being sanitized between uses. This is the one area that allows no shortcuts, because shared, uncleaned implements are how infections spread. Every tool must be disinfected or replaced between clients, every time, and disposable items like files and buffers should be new for each guest. Making that sanitation visible, with tools coming out of sealed pouches or a disinfectant, also reassures clients that their health is protected. It is the foundation everything else rests on.

## Editorial Perspective

The encouraging part is that the hardest thing to build, a friendly team, is already here, so the rest is about technique and standards. First, focus on adhesion so gel lasts: thorough prep, proper dehydration, careful application, and full curing, so a set holds for weeks instead of peeling in a day. Second, and without exception, sanitize hygiene: disinfect or replace every tool between clients, use fresh disposables, and make the cleaning visible so clients feel safe. With gel that lasts and tools that are always clean, this salon's warm service would translate into loyal customers, instead of a set that peeled off within days.
