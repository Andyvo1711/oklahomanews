Campus Corner Hair Studio in Stillwater has adjusted its staffing schedule and service menu to manage the sharp seasonal swings in demand that come with operating a salon in a college town closely tied to the Oklahoma State University academic calendar.

Salon ownership describes a business rhythm unlike a typical small-town salon, with demand surging around the start of the academic year, before major campus events, and around graduation, then dropping noticeably during summer break and other periods when much of the student population has left town. Managing staffing and inventory around those predictable but dramatic swings has required a more deliberate planning approach than a salon serving a more stable, non-seasonal customer base might need.

## Planning Staffing Around the Academic Calendar

The salon now adjusts its stylist schedule seasonally, bringing on additional part-time staff during peak periods tied to the start of each semester and major campus events, while scaling back hours during the quieter summer months when student demand drops significantly. Salon ownership describes this flexible staffing model as essential to remaining profitable year-round, rather than either overstaffing during slow periods or being unable to meet demand during predictable peak weeks.

Beyond staffing, the salon has also adjusted its service and product offerings to reflect its client base, which skews younger and more budget-conscious than salons serving a broader, non-college-town clientele. Ownership describes offering a range of price points and service lengths to accommodate students balancing salon visits against a tight budget, alongside services aimed at faculty, staff, and longtime Stillwater residents who make up a smaller but more consistent year-round customer base.

## Building Loyalty Across a Transient Customer Base

One of the ongoing challenges of operating in a college town, salon ownership says, is that a meaningful share of the customer base turns over every few years as students graduate and move away, requiring continuous new customer acquisition rather than relying solely on repeat business built up over many years. The salon has leaned on social media and campus-adjacent marketing to reach incoming students each year, treating customer acquisition as an ongoing, seasonal task rather than a one-time effort.

Looking ahead, ownership says they expect the salon's seasonal rhythm to remain a permanent feature of operating in Stillwater, and they've built their business planning process around anticipating those swings each year rather than treating any single slow season as an unusual or concerning downturn.
