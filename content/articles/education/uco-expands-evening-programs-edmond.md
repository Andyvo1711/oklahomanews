The University of Central Oklahoma has expanded its slate of evening and hybrid course offerings, part of a continuing effort to reach working adults in Edmond and the surrounding metro area who want to finish or begin a degree without stepping away from full-time employment. The added sections span several of the university's most in-demand programs, including business, nursing prerequisites, and general education requirements.

University administrators describe the expansion as a response to years of feedback from prospective students who found daytime-only scheduling incompatible with jobs, childcare, or both. Rather than treating evening courses as a secondary option layered on top of a traditional daytime schedule, the university has begun building some programs around evening and hybrid formats from the outset.

## Designing Around Real Schedules

Course planners say the shift required rethinking not just when classes meet but how they are structured. Hybrid sections combine in-person evening meetings with online coursework that students can complete on their own time, an arrangement intended to reduce the number of trips to campus required each week without eliminating face-to-face instruction entirely.

Faculty teaching in the expanded evening slate say the student population in these sections tends to differ meaningfully from traditional daytime classes, often including students returning to school after several years in the workforce, parents managing school-age children, and shift workers whose daytime hours are already committed elsewhere.

## Filling a Gap in Regional Access

Edmond and the broader Oklahoma City metro have a substantial population of adults who started college but did not finish, and university officials see the evening expansion as a direct way to reach that group. Rather than competing primarily for recent high school graduates, the added sections are aimed at a different kind of prospective student, one who may need more flexible advising and enrollment support alongside the schedule changes.

The university has also adjusted advising office hours to accommodate the new sections, recognizing that students attending evening classes often cannot meet with advisors during standard daytime hours either. Administrators say early enrollment in the new sections has been encouraging, though they caution that sustained success will depend on continued adjustments based on which formats and subjects draw the strongest demand.

Looking ahead, program leaders say they intend to evaluate the expanded offerings after a full academic year before deciding which additional subject areas might be added to the evening and hybrid rotation. For now, the university considers the effort a meaningful step toward making a four-year degree more accessible to adults across the metro area who have been balancing work and family responsibilities while considering a return to higher education.
