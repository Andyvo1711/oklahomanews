University of Oklahoma President Joseph Harroz Jr. used his annual State of the University addresses on Sept. 1 and 2 to highlight record enrollment, rising research activity and the early stages of a university-wide artificial intelligence framework, according to OU News.

## Enrollment and Research

Harroz said enrollment is nearing 40,000 and that OU expects to pass that mark next year. Health Campus enrollment has risen more than 31% in six years and has passed 4,000 students.

Research activity is up 70% in six years, with annual expenditures rising from about $250 million to about $550 million. The university's goal is $1 billion by 2031. A program called Project 200 aims to recruit about 200 research faculty, 150 of them on the Health Campus, which entered the nation's top 100 for NIH funding for the first time.

## OU Health, Fundraising and Accountability

OU Health's annual revenue grew from about $1.4 billion to nearly $4 billion, and patient visits rose from about 650,000 to more than 1 million a year. A 175,000-square-foot Stephenson Cancer Center is under construction in Tulsa.

OU is nearing completion of a $2 billion fundraising campaign and secured about $178 million in federal support this year. A Presidential Advisory Council on Accountable Shared Governance will convene to review shared governance.

On AI, Harroz said "we can't just boil the ocean," describing a framework covering teaching, research, health care and staff work. "We have to earn this every single day," he said.
