Tulsa Community College has rolled out a new advanced manufacturing certificate program, designed to give students a faster, more targeted path into skilled production roles than a traditional two-year associate degree. The certificate can be completed in significantly less time than a full degree program while still covering the core competencies employers in the region's manufacturing sector have identified as most essential.

College administrators say the new track responds directly to feedback from area manufacturers, who have described a persistent gap between the number of production positions requiring specialized technical skills and the number of job seekers with relevant training. Rather than assuming all manufacturing career interest translates into demand for a full associate degree, the certificate program targets students who want a quicker entry point into the workforce.

## Curriculum Built Around Equipment Literacy

The certificate coursework emphasizes practical competencies including precision measurement, quality control procedures, and familiarity with computer-controlled production equipment increasingly common across Tulsa-area manufacturing facilities. Faculty designed the program with input from regional employers to ensure graduates arrive with skills that map directly onto entry-level production roles rather than requiring extensive additional on-the-job training.

Students in the program complete a mix of classroom instruction and hands-on lab time using equipment similar to what they would encounter in an actual manufacturing environment. College officials say this hands-on component was a deliberate design choice, since manufacturing employers have consistently emphasized that classroom knowledge alone does not translate reliably into production floor competence.

## A Faster On-Ramp to Regional Industry

Tulsa's manufacturing base includes a mix of aerospace component production, general industrial manufacturing, and specialized fabrication operations, many of which have struggled to fill entry-level technical positions in recent years. College leaders describe the certificate program as one tool among several aimed at addressing that gap, alongside existing associate degree and apprenticeship pathways already offered through the college's technical programs.

Early enrollment in the certificate program has drawn a mix of recent high school graduates and adults seeking a career change, according to college officials, with several students citing the shorter time commitment as a key factor in their decision to enroll. The college has structured the program to allow graduates to later apply certificate coursework toward a full associate degree if they choose to continue their education.

Program administrators say they will track graduate employment outcomes closely during the certificate's first several cohorts, using that data to refine coursework and determine whether additional certificate tracks in adjacent manufacturing specialties might be warranted in future years.
