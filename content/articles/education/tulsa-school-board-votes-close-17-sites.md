The Tulsa Public Schools board voted 6-1 to approve a plan to close or consolidate 17 school sites, with Vice President Calvin Moniz casting the only dissenting vote, according to local news reports.

## The Decision

The plan covers 14 elementary schools, two middle schools and one alternative program. Sources differ slightly on timing: some describe the closures as coming after the 2026-27 school year, while others describe the changes as beginning with 2027-28.

The district frames the move as a response to under-use and a roughly $12 million budget shortfall. All affected sites enroll fewer than 400 students and use less than 65% of their buildings.

## Reaction

Families interviewed by local outlets said they were weighing options for where to send their children. Critics, including former board member Ken Malloy, called the closures an "institutional earthquake."

## What Happens Next

Savings are expected to come largely from staffing changes, and the district has said actual figures will depend on implementation costs. Residents will watch how the district handles transitions for students and staff, and what becomes of the buildings.
