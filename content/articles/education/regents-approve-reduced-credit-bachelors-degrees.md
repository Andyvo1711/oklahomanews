The Oklahoma State Regents for Higher Education on Sept. 3 approved four reduced-credit bachelor's degree programs, the state's first, at the University of Oklahoma, Oklahoma State University and Rogers State University.

## The Four Programs

The approved programs are OU's Bachelor of Applied Studies in Social Work, OSU's Bachelor of Applied Science degrees in artificial intelligence and in business essentials and applications, and Rogers State's Bachelor of Applied Science in workforce development. They require between 90 and 103 credit hours, compared with a traditional minimum of 120.

## How the Framework Works

The regents set up a framework for reduced-credit degrees in May, following a feasibility study directed by House Bill 3315, authored by Rep. Ryan Eaves and Sen. Julie Daniels. Institutions seeking approval had to demonstrate workforce and student demand and address a workforce gap while keeping learning outcomes and accreditation standards intact.

## What Comes Next

The four programs still need approval from the Higher Learning Commission before students can enroll. Supporters say shorter degrees can lower costs and speed entry into the workforce, while the regents' requirements are meant to ensure the programs do not dilute academic quality. Oklahoma joins a growing number of states that have approved three-year bachelor's options.
