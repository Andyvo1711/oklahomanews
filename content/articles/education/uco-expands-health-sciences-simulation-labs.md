The University of Central Oklahoma has expanded its clinical simulation labs in Edmond, adding new equipment and lab space to accommodate steady enrollment growth in its nursing and allied health programs over the past several admissions cycles.

Simulation labs use programmable mannequins and staged clinical scenarios to let nursing and allied health students practice patient care skills in a controlled setting before they encounter real patients during clinical rotations. UCO faculty say the technology has become an essential part of health sciences education, partly because hospital clinical placement slots have grown harder to secure as more area colleges compete for the same limited number of spots at regional health systems.

## Filling a Clinical Placement Gap

Nursing programs across the state have described a similar bottleneck in recent years: enrollment in nursing programs has grown as students respond to a well-publicized nursing shortage, but the number of clinical placement slots at area hospitals has not grown at the same pace. Simulation labs don't fully replace hands-on clinical rotations, but they let students build and demonstrate core competencies before they ever set foot on a hospital floor, which faculty say makes limited clinical placement time more efficient once it does happen.

UCO's expanded lab space includes additional simulation rooms designed to mimic a range of care settings, from a standard hospital room to an emergency intake area, allowing instructors to rotate different student cohorts through varied scenarios without waiting for a single lab to free up.

## Preparing for Continued Enrollment Growth

University officials say the lab expansion was planned in response to admissions data showing consistent, multi-year growth in applications to UCO's nursing and allied health tracks, a trend they expect to continue given ongoing regional demand for healthcare workers. Faculty involved in the expansion say the investment is also aimed at improving how students perform once they do reach clinical rotations, since simulation training gives students repeated, low-stakes practice with procedures they might otherwise only see once or twice in a real clinical setting.

The university has not announced further expansion plans beyond the current project, but administrators say lab capacity will remain a factor they track closely as enrollment in health sciences programs continues to outpace the university's overall growth rate. For prospective students weighing nursing programs across the Oklahoma City metro, the expanded simulation space is likely to be one of the more visible differences between UCO's program and competing options nearby.
