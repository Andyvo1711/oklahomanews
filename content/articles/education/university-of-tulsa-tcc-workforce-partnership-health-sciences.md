Tulsa Community College and the University of Tulsa have signed a new articulation agreement that lets students move from an associate degree in health sciences directly into bachelor's-level coursework, with a guarantee that core credits will transfer without duplication.

The agreement targets a persistent frustration for transfer students in Oklahoma: credits that count toward an associate degree at a community college sometimes don't map cleanly onto a four-year program's requirements, forcing students to repeat coursework and add semesters they can't afford. Under the new pathway, TCC students in designated pre-health tracks will follow a jointly mapped course sequence from their first semester, so advisors on both campuses can confirm in advance that classes will count on the other end.

## Addressing a Regional Workforce Gap

Tulsa-area hospitals and clinics have spent much of the past two years describing a shortage of health professionals in specific roles, from respiratory therapy to medical laboratory science, and both institutions frame the partnership partly as a response to that need. TCC's lower-cost, open-access model has long served as an entry point for students who might not otherwise consider a health sciences career, while the University of Tulsa offers upper-division coursework and clinical placements that TCC does not.

Administrators from both schools say the partnership was built around joint advising sessions, where a TCC counselor and a TU admissions representative meet with prospective transfer students together, rather than students navigating two separate offices on their own. That single-point-of-contact approach is meant to catch scheduling conflicts or missing prerequisites well before a student is ready to transfer.

## What Students Can Expect

Students entering the pathway will still apply to the University of Tulsa as transfer students, and admission is not automatic. But the joint course mapping means a student who follows the recommended sequence at TCC should arrive with the specific prerequisite courses TU's health science programs expect, reducing the likelihood of being asked to retake general education or introductory science courses.

Both schools plan to review the pathway's early cohorts over the next two years, tracking how many students who start at TCC actually complete the transfer and finish a bachelor's degree on time. For a region trying to rebuild its pipeline of health professionals from the ground up, officials say the partnership is a modest but concrete step toward keeping more students on a single, affordable route from associate to bachelor's degree.
