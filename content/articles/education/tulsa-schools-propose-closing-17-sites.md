Tulsa Public Schools on Sept. 21 laid out a proposal to close or consolidate 17 school sites, a plan the district says would save about $12 million.

## What the Plan Covers

The proposal involves 14 elementary schools, two middle schools and one alternative program. Sites named in reporting include Central Middle School and Tulsa MET Middle and High School. District officials say every site in the proposal enrolls fewer than 400 students and has building use below 65%.

## Savings and Open Questions

Reporting on the plan puts core savings at about $11.1 million, including $8.5 million from staffing changes and $2.5 million in operational savings, with the board also directing the central office to find an additional $1.5 million. One outlet noted that actual savings will depend on implementation costs, staffing decisions and the expense of preparing receiving schools.

About $7.4 million from Tulsa's 2021 bond program remains earmarked for renovations at Sequoyah Elementary, one of the sites slated to close, a complication that raised questions during discussion.

## Families Weigh In

The proposal drew immediate attention from families whose neighborhood schools could be affected. The district said the changes are intended to put resources into classrooms rather than underused buildings. A formal board vote followed in October.
