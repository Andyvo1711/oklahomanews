Rose State College has expanded its scholarship fund dedicated to first-generation college students, adding new award tiers intended to cover a broader range of costs beyond tuition alone. College officials say the expansion reflects growing recognition that tuition assistance by itself often fails to address the full scope of financial pressure facing first-generation students, many of whom also contend with textbook costs, transportation, and reduced ability to work while enrolled.

The Midwest City-based community college has for years maintained scholarship support targeted at first-generation students, but administrators say the fund's growth allows it to reach a larger number of students each academic year and to provide awards that better reflect the real cost of attendance.

## Addressing Costs Beyond Tuition

Financial aid staff at the college say first-generation students often face unique challenges navigating the financial aid process itself, in addition to the underlying cost burden. Without family members who have gone through the college financial aid system before, these students may miss deadlines, misunderstand award letters, or fail to apply for supplemental aid they would otherwise qualify for.

To address this, the expanded scholarship program includes a stronger advising component, pairing scholarship recipients with staff who help them navigate not just the scholarship application itself but the broader financial aid landscape, including federal aid renewal requirements and emergency assistance resources available through the college.

## A Community College Approach to Access

Rose State's approach reflects a broader identity many community colleges have embraced as accessible entry points into higher education, particularly for students whose family circumstances make a four-year university's cost structure prohibitive from the outset. College leaders describe the scholarship expansion as consistent with that mission, aiming to remove financial barriers that might otherwise cause capable students to delay or abandon their education.

Students who have received scholarships through the fund have described the support as significant not only financially but in terms of confidence, knowing that the college recognizes and actively supports the particular challenges first-generation students face. College officials say they plan to track completion rates among scholarship recipients compared to the broader first-generation student population to assess the program's long-term impact.

The college has indicated it intends to continue growing the fund through ongoing fundraising efforts, with a goal of eventually reaching a larger share of eligible first-generation students than the program currently serves. Administrators describe the expansion as an incremental, multi-year effort rather than a single completed initiative.
