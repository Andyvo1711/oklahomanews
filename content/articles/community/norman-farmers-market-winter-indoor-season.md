The Norman farmers market has launched an indoor winter season for the first time in its history, moving into a rented community space to give local growers and small food producers a year-round sales outlet after decades of operating on a strictly seasonal outdoor schedule.

Market organizers say the decision to add a winter season came after years of requests from vendors who produce goods that don't fit neatly into a April-through-October outdoor market schedule, including bakers, small-batch food producers, and growers with winter crops like greens and root vegetables that are ready for market during the colder months.

## Meeting Vendor Demand for Winter Sales

Several longtime market vendors had previously sold at winter markets in other cities or through online ordering systems during Norman's off-season, arrangements market organizers say were workable but inconvenient compared with having a consistent local venue. The new indoor winter season gives those vendors a predictable, twice-monthly opportunity to sell directly to Norman customers without needing to travel to other cities during the winter months.

Attendance at the first few winter market dates has been smaller than the market's peak summer outdoor crowds, which organizers say was expected given the different format and reduced vendor lineup compared with the full outdoor season. Even with smaller crowds, participating vendors describe the winter market as a valuable addition, providing steady, if modest, sales during months that previously generated no direct-to-consumer revenue at all.

## Building Toward a Sustainable Year-Round Model

Market organizers say they're evaluating attendance and vendor feedback from this first winter season to determine whether the indoor market should continue in future years or whether adjustments to format, frequency, or location might improve turnout. The rented indoor space comes with additional costs compared with the market's usual outdoor site, and organizers have said covering those costs sustainably will be a key factor in the winter market's long-term viability.

For now, market organizers describe the winter season as a worthwhile experiment that has already strengthened relationships with vendors who appreciate having a local, direct-to-consumer sales option available even during Norman's colder months, when the outdoor market has traditionally gone dormant entirely.
