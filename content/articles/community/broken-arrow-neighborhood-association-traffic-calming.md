A neighborhood association in Broken Arrow has worked with city traffic engineers to install speed bumps and improved crosswalk markings near a local elementary school, following months of resident concerns about cut-through traffic using neighborhood streets to bypass a congested main road during school pickup and drop-off hours.

Residents first raised the issue at a neighborhood association meeting last year, describing near-miss incidents involving children walking to school and drivers using residential streets as a shortcut around the more congested main thoroughfare during peak hours. The association organized a petition and worked with the city's traffic engineering department to study the intersection and surrounding streets before any changes were approved.

## From Resident Complaints to City Action

City traffic engineers conducted a traffic study over several weeks, counting vehicle speeds and cut-through volume on the affected streets before recommending a combination of speed bumps and updated crosswalk signage. Neighborhood association leaders say the process took longer than some residents would have liked, but credit the city's methodical approach with ensuring the eventual changes were based on actual traffic data rather than anecdotal complaints alone.

The installed measures include raised speed bumps at two points along the most heavily used cut-through route, along with brighter crosswalk markings and additional signage near the elementary school's main pedestrian crossing. Neighborhood leaders say early observation suggests cut-through traffic has decreased noticeably since the changes were installed, though the association plans to request a formal follow-up traffic count from the city to confirm the trend with actual data.

## A Model for Other Neighborhoods

Association leaders say they've fielded interest from residents in other Broken Arrow neighborhoods facing similar cut-through traffic concerns, and have shared their experience working through the city's traffic study process as a rough template other groups might follow. City officials have said they welcome similar organized requests from other neighborhoods, though they note that traffic-calming measures are evaluated individually based on each location's specific data rather than applied uniformly across the city.

For families near the elementary school, association leaders describe the changes as a meaningful, if incremental, improvement to a safety concern that had worried parents for some time, crediting sustained resident organizing with turning a recurring complaint into a concrete infrastructure change.
