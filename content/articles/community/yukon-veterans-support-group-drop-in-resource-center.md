A veterans support group in Yukon has opened a drop-in resource center offering help navigating benefits paperwork alongside informal peer support, giving local veterans and their families a walk-in option that doesn't require an appointment or a lengthy application process to access basic help.

Organizers behind the center say the idea grew out of recognizing how many veterans in the community struggled simply to understand which benefits they were eligible for and how to complete the associated paperwork, a process that can be confusing even for veterans who are otherwise comfortable navigating other government systems.

## Simplifying Access to Benefits Support

The resource center is staffed largely by volunteers, many of them veterans themselves, who have gone through training on how to help walk-in visitors with common benefits questions and paperwork, though the center refers more complex cases to accredited veterans service officers rather than attempting to handle every situation informally. Organizers describe the center's role as a first stop that can answer basic questions quickly and connect veterans to the right formal resources when a situation requires more specialized help.

Beyond paperwork assistance, the center has also become an informal gathering space where veterans can talk with peers who understand shared experiences, something organizers say has proven valuable in its own right, separate from any specific benefits question a visitor might have come in with originally.

## Building a Sustainable Community Resource

The center currently operates on limited hours, staffed by volunteers around their own schedules, and organizers say expanding hours will depend on recruiting additional trained volunteers willing to commit regular time to the effort. Local businesses and community groups have provided some funding and in-kind support to cover the center's basic operating costs, including rent for the small space it currently occupies.

Organizers describe early response from the local veteran community as strong, with steady walk-in traffic since opening, and they say they're optimistic about the center's ability to grow into a more consistently available resource as volunteer capacity and funding allow, filling what they describe as a real gap in accessible, informal support for veterans in the Yukon area.
