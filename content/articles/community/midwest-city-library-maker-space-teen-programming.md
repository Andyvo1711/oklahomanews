The Midwest City public library has converted an underused meeting room into a maker space equipped with 3D printers, sewing machines, and basic electronics kits, giving area teenagers a free after-school destination that librarians say has quickly become one of the branch's most consistently used spaces.

Library staff say the idea grew out of repeated requests from teens and parents for programming beyond traditional homework help and study space, particularly during the after-school hours before working parents get home. The maker space is staffed by librarians trained on the equipment, along with occasional volunteers from a local robotics club who help teens with more advanced electronics projects.

## Filling an After-School Gap

For many families in Midwest City, the hours between school dismissal and a parent's return from work represent a stretch of unsupervised time that can be difficult to fill productively, especially for families without the budget for structured after-school programs or childcare. Library staff describe the maker space as a low-cost answer to that gap, offering teens a supervised, engaging place to spend those hours without any membership fee or registration requirement beyond a library card.

Usage patterns have shown steady growth since the space opened, with librarians noting that many of the same teens return several times a week, developing ongoing projects rather than treating the space as a one-time novelty. Popular projects have included simple 3D-printed household items, basic sewing projects, and small robotics builds guided by the visiting robotics club volunteers.

## Expanding Access Through Partnerships

Library administrators say they're exploring partnerships with the school district to make sure students who might benefit most from the space know it exists, particularly students who may not have other structured after-school options available to them. The library has also fielded interest from other library branches in the metro area curious about replicating the model, though administrators note that space and staffing constraints make it difficult for every branch to add similar equipment right away.

For now, library officials describe the maker space as one of the more successful recent additions to their teen programming, citing consistent attendance and positive feedback from both teens and parents as evidence that the investment in equipment and staff time has paid off in a meaningful way for the community.
