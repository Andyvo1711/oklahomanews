Oklahoma Heart Hospital has expanded the capacity of its cardiac rehabilitation program, adding additional supervised exercise sessions and staff to reduce wait times that had grown for patients referred to the program following heart procedures or cardiac events. Hospital administrators say the expansion responds to sustained demand that had outpaced the program's previous scheduling capacity.

Cardiac rehabilitation programs combine supervised exercise, education, and monitoring for patients recovering from heart attacks, bypass surgery, or other cardiac interventions, and research has consistently supported their role in reducing the likelihood of future cardiac events. Hospital officials say ensuring timely access to the program, rather than leaving patients waiting weeks to begin, is an important factor in how effective the rehabilitation ultimately proves to be.

## Reducing the Gap Between Referral and Enrollment

Prior to the expansion, some patients faced waits between hospital discharge and the start of formal cardiac rehabilitation, a gap that clinical staff say can reduce patient motivation and engagement with the program. By adding additional session slots and staff capacity, hospital officials aim to shorten that window significantly, getting patients into structured rehabilitation closer to the point of discharge when motivation to make lifestyle changes tends to be highest.

Staff overseeing the expanded program say scheduling flexibility has also improved, with more session times available across different parts of the day to accommodate patients who are still working or managing family responsibilities during their recovery period.

## A Broader Focus on Cardiac Recovery

Hospital officials frame the rehabilitation expansion as part of a broader institutional focus on the full arc of cardiac care, not just the acute treatment phase but the months of recovery and lifestyle adjustment that follow. Clinical staff work with patients on structured exercise plans, nutrition guidance, and stress management strategies, with the goal of supporting sustainable long-term changes rather than a short-term recovery period alone.

The expanded program has also placed additional emphasis on patient education, helping participants understand their specific cardiac condition and the reasoning behind recommended lifestyle changes, an approach hospital staff say improves long-term adherence compared to generic recovery instructions.

Hospital administrators say they will continue monitoring enrollment and wait times following the expansion, with further capacity increases possible if demand continues to grow. They describe the investment as consistent with the hospital's broader mission of supporting patients through the complete cardiac care journey rather than ending involvement once the acute treatment phase concludes.
