Mercy has placed licensed mental health counselors directly inside several school districts across the Oklahoma City suburbs, an initiative designed to reach students who might never otherwise access counseling outside of a school setting.

School-based mental health programs have grown increasingly common nationally as districts and health systems try to address a documented rise in anxiety, depression, and other mental health concerns among school-age children and teenagers. Health system officials involved in the program say placing counselors inside school buildings removes some of the most common barriers to care, including transportation, scheduling conflicts with a parent's work hours, and the stigma some families associate with visiting an outside mental health clinic.

## Meeting Students Where They Are

Counselors embedded in participating schools work alongside school counselors and administrators, but maintain a distinct clinical role focused on ongoing therapy rather than the academic and scheduling support a traditional school counselor typically provides. Students can be referred by a teacher, school counselor, or parent, and sessions generally take place during the school day in a private space set aside for that purpose.

Mercy officials say the embedded model has proven especially valuable for families who might not have insurance coverage, reliable transportation, or the flexibility to take a child to a separate mental health appointment during business hours. By billing insurance through the health system directly and absorbing some costs for uninsured students through community benefit funding, the program aims to remove cost as a barrier for as many families as possible.

## Measuring Impact Over Time

School administrators involved in the partnership say early feedback from teachers has been positive, with some reporting fewer disruptive incidents in classrooms among students receiving regular counseling support, though officials caution that it is still early to draw firm conclusions about the program's broader impact on academic performance or school climate. Mercy plans to track engagement and outcome data as more students move through the program across a full school year.

The health system has said it hopes to expand the embedded counselor model to additional school districts if funding allows, though it is currently limited to a handful of partner districts in the Oklahoma City suburbs. For families in those districts, officials say the biggest immediate change has simply been how quickly a struggling student can be connected to care, often within days rather than the weeks or months a typical outside referral might take.
