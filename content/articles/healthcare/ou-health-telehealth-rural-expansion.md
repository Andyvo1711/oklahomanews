OU Health has expanded its telehealth network to reach additional rural communities across Oklahoma, part of a continuing effort to reduce the travel burden many patients face when seeking specialist care unavailable in their home counties. The expansion adds virtual consultation capacity in several specialty areas, allowing patients in rural clinics to connect with specialists based in Oklahoma City without making the long drive themselves.

Health system administrators describe telehealth expansion as one of the more practical tools available for addressing Oklahoma's uneven distribution of medical specialists, which remain concentrated in the state's two largest metro areas. For patients in rural counties, a specialist appointment has often meant a half-day or full-day commitment once travel time is factored in, a burden that can lead some patients to delay or skip care altogether.

## Extending Specialist Reach Without Relocation

Rather than attempting to recruit more specialists into rural practice, an approach that has had limited success across the state for years, the telehealth expansion works by connecting existing OU Health specialists with patients through partner clinics equipped with the necessary video technology. Local clinic staff handle in-person elements of the visit, including basic vital signs and any necessary physical examination support, while the specialist consultation happens remotely.

Health system officials say this hybrid model, combining local staff support with remote specialist expertise, has proven more sustainable than fully remote-only consultations, since patients still benefit from in-person clinical support during the visit itself. The approach has been applied across several specialty areas where rural access has historically been most limited.

## Building Trust in Remote Care

Adoption of telehealth services among rural patients has grown gradually rather than immediately, according to health system staff, with some patients initially hesitant about receiving specialist care without an in-person visit. Clinic staff have worked to address that hesitation through direct conversations about how the technology works and what patients can expect from a virtual consultation compared to a traditional office visit.

Administrators note that telehealth is not a replacement for all forms of specialist care, particularly for conditions requiring hands-on procedures or diagnostic testing unavailable at rural clinics. Instead, the expanded network is positioned as a complement to existing referral pathways, offering an additional option for patients whose conditions can be appropriately managed through virtual consultation.

OU Health officials say they plan to continue adding partner clinic sites as demand and technology infrastructure allow, with particular attention to counties that have seen reduced access to specialist care in recent years. The health system is also evaluating patient feedback from the current expansion to determine which specialty areas might benefit from additional telehealth capacity going forward.
