A network of community health clinics serving southern Oklahoma has expanded its footprint, opening additional sliding-scale primary care sites in communities along the Red River where access to affordable healthcare has historically been limited. The clinics serve patients regardless of ability to pay, with fees adjusted based on household income, a model designed to reach uninsured and underinsured residents who might otherwise delay care.

Clinic administrators say the expansion reflects sustained demand that existing sites could not fully accommodate, with some locations reporting appointment wait times that stretched longer than clinic leadership considered acceptable for a primary care access point intended to serve as a safety net for the region.

## Filling Gaps in Primary Care Access

Southern Oklahoma includes a mix of small towns and rural areas where primary care providers have become increasingly scarce, a pattern consistent with broader rural healthcare access challenges across the state. Community clinic leaders say their sliding-scale model fills a specific gap for patients without employer-sponsored insurance or the means to pay standard fees at a private practice.

Clinic staff emphasize that primary care access has ripple effects across the broader healthcare system, since patients without a regular primary care relationship are more likely to delay treatment for manageable conditions until they require more costly emergency care. By expanding access to routine primary care, clinic administrators hope to reduce that pattern among the populations they serve.

## Staffing a Growing Network

Expanding clinic capacity has required recruiting additional physicians, nurse practitioners, and support staff willing to work in a sliding-scale clinic setting, which typically offers lower compensation than private practice but appeals to providers drawn to community-focused care. Clinic leadership says recruitment has been an ongoing challenge, though partnerships with regional nursing and medical training programs have helped identify candidates interested in community health work.

The clinics have also expanded behavioral health and basic dental referral services at select locations, recognizing that patients accessing primary care in underserved areas often have unmet needs beyond general medical care that go unaddressed without accessible referral pathways.

Network administrators say they plan to continue evaluating additional communities for potential new clinic sites based on demonstrated need and available funding, describing the expansion as an ongoing response to persistent gaps in the region's healthcare access rather than a finite, time-limited project.
