Hillcrest HealthCare System has expanded its partnerships with schools across the Tulsa area, placing behavioral health staff on-site to provide mental wellness screening and streamlined referrals to counseling services. The expansion reflects growing recognition among healthcare providers that addressing student mental health requires meeting young people where they already spend the majority of their day, rather than relying solely on families to seek out services independently.

Health system administrators say the school-based approach has grown out of years of conversations with district staff about the difficulty many families face navigating the broader mental health referral system, including long wait times for outside appointments and uncertainty about which providers accept their insurance.

## Screening as an Early Intervention Tool

Behavioral health staff working within participating schools conduct voluntary screenings designed to identify students who may benefit from additional support, ranging from short-term counseling to more structured therapeutic intervention. Staff say the goal of screening is not to diagnose conditions on the spot but to identify students who warrant further evaluation, connecting them with appropriate resources more quickly than might otherwise happen.

School counselors and health system staff coordinate closely on these referrals, with health system clinicians providing more specialized behavioral health expertise that most school counseling departments are not staffed to offer independently given the volume of general counseling needs they already manage.

## Reducing Barriers to Follow-Through

One of the persistent challenges in adolescent mental health care has been the gap between identifying a need and a family actually following through on an outside referral, whether due to cost, scheduling, or logistical barriers. By embedding services directly within schools, health system officials say they hope to reduce some of that drop-off, since students can access initial support without requiring a parent to arrange transportation to an outside appointment during work hours.

Health system leaders emphasize that the school partnerships are not intended to replace family involvement or outside specialized care when needed, but rather to serve as an accessible entry point that makes it easier for students and families to engage with mental health support in the first place.

Hillcrest officials say the partnership program has expanded gradually, school by school, based on individual district interest and capacity to host on-site staff. They anticipate continued growth in the number of participating schools as awareness of the program spreads among Tulsa-area families and school administrators.
