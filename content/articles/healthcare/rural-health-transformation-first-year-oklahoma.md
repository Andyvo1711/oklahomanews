The Oklahoma State Department of Health on Sept. 29 held a media briefing on the first-year progress of the Rural Health Transformation Program, with Gov. Kevin Stitt and Commissioner of Health Keith Reed taking part.

## The Funding

Oklahoma received more than $223 million from the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services for the program's first year, part of a five-year, $50 billion federal investment meant to transform rural health care nationwide.

## On Track With Deadlines

The department's release said the state is on track to meet CMS spending deadlines. Projects supported by the funding include a certified nurse midwife and new clinics in Shattuck and Woodward, part of an effort that also touches Newman Memorial Hospital's plan to restart maternity care.

## The Tension

Rural hospital leaders have said the funding is welcome but arrives alongside Medicaid reductions. The Oklahoma Hospital Association has projected a large drop in Medicaid funding for state hospitals over the next decade, while several rural facilities, including Ascension St. John Nowata, are moving to rural emergency hospital status.
