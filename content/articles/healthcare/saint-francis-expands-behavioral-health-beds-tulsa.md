Saint Francis Health System has added inpatient behavioral health beds at its Tulsa campus, a response to a shortage that has, in some cases, left patients waiting in emergency departments for days before a psychiatric bed becomes available anywhere in the region.

Emergency department "boarding," the practice of holding a patient in the ED because no inpatient bed exists elsewhere in the system, has become a familiar frustration for hospital administrators nationally, and behavioral health patients are frequently among those affected most. A patient in psychiatric crisis often needs a specialized unit rather than a general medical bed, and when no such unit has an opening, the emergency department becomes a holding pattern that can stretch on for days.

## Addressing a Regional Bed Shortage

Health system administrators describe the new beds as a direct response to internal data showing average boarding times for behavioral health patients had climbed steadily over recent years, a trend mirrored at other Tulsa-area hospitals. The expansion adds capacity specifically for adult psychiatric patients, an area where demand has outpaced the region's existing bed count for some time.

Behavioral health staffing has been one of the more difficult pieces to solve alongside the physical bed expansion. Psychiatric units require nurses and technicians trained specifically in de-escalation and psychiatric care, a workforce that has been in short supply nationally. Saint Francis has paired the new beds with a recruitment push aimed at psychiatric nursing staff, along with expanded training for general medical-surgical nurses who may rotate through behavioral health units during peak demand periods.

## A Piece of a Larger System Problem

Hospital leaders are careful to frame the expansion as one piece of a larger, region-wide challenge rather than a complete solution. Outpatient psychiatric capacity, community mental health resources, and crisis stabilization services all factor into whether patients end up in an emergency department at all, and Saint Francis officials say they continue to work with community mental health partners on diverting patients to outpatient care when inpatient hospitalization isn't clinically necessary.

For now, the added beds are expected to reduce, though not eliminate, the boarding delays that have frustrated both patients and emergency department staff. Health system officials say they will monitor bed utilization and boarding time data over the coming months to determine whether further expansion is warranted, particularly as the Tulsa area's population continues to grow.
