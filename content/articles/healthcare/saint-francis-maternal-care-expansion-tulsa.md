Saint Francis Health System has expanded prenatal and postpartum support services across its Tulsa-area facilities, adding staff capacity and extending the timeline of structured postpartum follow-up available to new mothers. The expansion comes as maternal health outcomes have drawn increased attention across Oklahoma, with rural and underserved communities facing particular access challenges.

Health system administrators describe the expansion as addressing gaps identified through internal review of patient outcomes and feedback, particularly around the postpartum period, when many new mothers previously had limited structured contact with their care team after leaving the hospital following delivery.

## Extending Support Beyond Delivery

The expanded program adds additional postpartum check-in appointments beyond the traditional six-week follow-up visit, giving new mothers more touchpoints with clinical staff during a period when complications including postpartum depression and physical recovery challenges are most likely to emerge. Health system officials say this extended follow-up model reflects a broader shift in maternal care philosophy toward treating the postpartum period as an extended window requiring ongoing attention rather than a single follow-up visit.

Nurses and care coordinators involved in the expanded program work to identify warning signs during these additional visits, referring patients to appropriate specialists when needed rather than waiting for problems to escalate to the point of emergency care. Health system leaders say this proactive approach is intended to catch complications earlier, when treatment options tend to be less invasive and more effective.

## Addressing a Statewide Concern

Maternal health outcomes have become a subject of sustained attention among Oklahoma healthcare providers and policymakers, with particular concern about disparities affecting rural and underserved communities. Saint Francis officials say the Tulsa-area expansion is one piece of a broader effort among the state's health systems to address these gaps, though they acknowledge that meaningful statewide improvement will require sustained investment from multiple institutions over time.

The health system has also expanded lactation support services and added staff dedicated specifically to helping new mothers navigate the transition home after delivery, including connecting families with community resources for those facing additional social or economic pressures during the postpartum period.

Saint Francis administrators say they will continue monitoring outcomes from the expanded program, with an eye toward identifying which components have the most measurable impact on maternal health outcomes in the Tulsa area. The health system has indicated openness to expanding similar programming to additional facilities depending on results from the current initiative.
