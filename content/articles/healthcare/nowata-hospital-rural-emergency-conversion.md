Ascension St. John has applied to reclassify its Nowata hospital as a Rural Emergency Hospital, a designation that would end inpatient care while keeping emergency and outpatient services, and many residents are fighting the move.

## Ascension's Case

The system says inpatient admissions at Nowata have fallen 58% since 2020, with an average daily inpatient census of 4.85 patients over the past decade. Patients who need hospitalization would be stabilized and transferred to the nearest appropriate hospital. Ascension says the hospital would maintain 24/7 emergency care and outpatient services, and a September announcement said it will continue serving Nowata and surrounding rural communities under the new model.

## Community Opposition

A group called Save Our Hospital formed over the summer, holding gatherings and circulating a petition asking that the conversion be withdrawn, denied or suspended pending further review. The application was reviewed by the Oklahoma State Department of Health and the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services.

## A Growing Trend

Rural emergency hospital status, a federal designation available since 2023, lets small hospitals keep emergency departments while shedding inpatient beds. Opponents warn it can reduce access for older and sicker patients, who may face longer trips for admission.
