Oklahoma Primary Aluminum released an economic impact study in late September projecting about $49 billion in cumulative impact through 2060 for its proposed smelter at the Tulsa Port of Inola in Rogers County.

## What the Study Says

The study, by Regional Economic Models, Inc., estimates the facility could support an average of about 7,000 Oklahoma jobs a year, peaking at 10,600 in 2029. It counts more than 1,000 permanent direct jobs and more than 5,000 indirect and induced jobs. The project, a more than $4 billion investment on 441 acres, would produce 750,000 metric tons of aluminum annually. Civil works are penciled in for the second quarter of 2027, with first hot metal in mid- to third-quarter 2030.

## Local Hurdles

The Inola city council extended a moratorium on smelter permits and called a referendum on the project. Gov. Kevin Stitt has said the industrial park could be moved outside the city if the council keeps opposing it. The study is the company's own, and a letter from President Trump cited a lower $26.3 billion estimate.

## Ownership

The project is a joint venture of EGA (60%) and Century Aluminum (40%). Attorney General Gentner Drummond filed a petition in June seeking to block it, and an air quality permit is under state review.
