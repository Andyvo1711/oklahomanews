Commercial real estate brokers in Lawton describe steady activity along the corridor connecting the city to Fort Sill, with retail, dining, and service businesses continuing to lease space aimed at serving the substantial military and civilian workforce population tied to the installation.

Lawton's commercial market has historically moved in close relationship with activity at Fort Sill, and brokers say that connection remains a defining feature of the local commercial real estate landscape, with businesses often designing their location strategy specifically around proximity to the base and the housing patterns of military families in the surrounding area.

## A Market Shaped by Military Population Patterns

Unlike many commercial markets that track primarily with broader regional population and income growth, Lawton's retail and service sector leasing activity is closely tied to Fort Sill's troop levels and the housing choices of military families, many of whom rent rather than own and may relocate every few years as part of standard military reassignment cycles. Commercial brokers say this creates a market with more turnover in both retail tenants and residential renters than a typical mid-sized Oklahoma city, but also a level of built-in demand that has proven relatively resilient over time.

Businesses that cater specifically to military families, including certain retail categories and service providers familiar with military benefits and scheduling needs, have continued to find steady demand along the corridor, according to brokers tracking leasing activity in the area.

## Diversification Beyond Fort Sill

Local economic development officials have said for years that reducing the city's economic dependence on Fort Sill remains a long-term goal, and some recent commercial activity reflects that push, including new logistics and light manufacturing tenants exploring space in and around Lawton. Brokers describe this diversification as still in its early stages, with Fort Sill and its associated workforce remaining the dominant driver of commercial real estate demand along the core corridor for the foreseeable future.

Commercial real estate professionals in the market say they expect continued steady, if unspectacular, leasing activity along the corridor, with any significant shift likely tied either to changes in Fort Sill's mission and troop levels or to the success of ongoing efforts to attract employers unrelated to the military installation.
