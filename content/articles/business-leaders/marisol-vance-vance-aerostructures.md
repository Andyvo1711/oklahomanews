Vance Aerostructures, a precision components supplier based on Oklahoma City's east side, has grown steadily since founder Marisol Vance launched the company from a leased warehouse bay a decade ago. What began as a small operation supplying fasteners and simple brackets to larger aerospace contractors has expanded into a facility producing more complex machined components for maintenance and manufacturing clients across the region.

Vance, a mechanical engineer by training, started the company after several years working for a larger aerospace supplier where she oversaw quality control processes. She has said in past interviews with local business publications that she saw an opportunity to serve smaller aerospace clients who felt underserved by larger suppliers focused primarily on high-volume contracts.

## Growing Through Specialization

Rather than competing broadly across the aerospace supply chain, Vance built the company's reputation around a narrower specialization in precision-machined components requiring tight tolerances, a niche that allowed the young company to compete for contracts based on quality rather than price alone against larger, more established suppliers.

That strategy has paid off gradually, with the company expanding its client base to include both maintenance and repair operations and smaller manufacturing contracts. The Oklahoma City facility has added machining capacity twice since its founding, most recently completing an expansion that added several new computer-controlled machining stations and additional floor space for quality testing.

## Investing in Local Talent

As Vance Aerostructures has grown, the company has placed particular emphasis on hiring and training workers from within the Oklahoma City area rather than recruiting extensively from outside the region. The company has partnered with local technical training programs to identify candidates with foundational machining skills who can be trained further on the company's specific equipment and quality standards.

Company leadership describes this approach as both a practical necessity, given the tight labor market for skilled machinists, and a deliberate choice to build a workforce with roots in the local community. Several of the company's current machinists began as trainees hired directly out of regional career and technology programs.

Looking ahead, Vance has said the company plans to continue growing its capacity gradually rather than pursuing rapid expansion that might outpace its ability to maintain the quality standards that have defined its client relationships. The company continues to evaluate additional facility investments as its client base grows, with an emphasis on sustainable, incremental growth over aggressive expansion.
