Wheatland Ag Robotics, an Enid-based agricultural technology company founded by Derek Ainsworth, has spent the past several years developing automated attachments designed to help small and mid-sized wheat operations reduce labor costs without requiring the full replacement of existing equipment. The company's approach centers on retrofitting existing tractors and implements rather than selling entirely new machinery, a strategy Ainsworth has said reflects the economic reality facing many family farming operations in the region.

Ainsworth, who grew up on a wheat farm before pursuing a career in robotics engineering, returned to western Oklahoma after several years working in the automation industry elsewhere. He founded the company with the goal of bringing more affordable automation technology to operations that could not justify the cost of large-scale precision agriculture systems marketed primarily to bigger farms.

## Retrofitting Over Replacing

The company's core product line includes sensor and guidance attachments that can be added to existing equipment, allowing smaller operations to gain some of the efficiency benefits of automated guidance and monitoring without the capital expense of purchasing entirely new precision agriculture systems. Ainsworth has described this retrofit approach as central to the company's identity, distinguishing it from larger agricultural technology firms that primarily target large-scale operations.

Company engineers work closely with individual farm operators to understand their specific equipment and field conditions, an approach that has meant slower scaling than a more standardized product line might allow, but one Ainsworth has said produces better outcomes for the company's core customer base of smaller wheat producers.

## Building a Team Rooted in Agriculture

Wheatland Ag Robotics has grown its engineering team gradually, prioritizing hires who bring both technical robotics experience and a working understanding of agricultural operations. Ainsworth has said this combination of skills is harder to find than pure engineering talent alone, since understanding the practical realities of farm equipment use in the field matters as much as the underlying technology itself.

The company has also built relationships with regional equipment dealers who help identify potential customers and provide installation support for the company's retrofit products, an arrangement that has allowed Wheatland Ag Robotics to expand its reach without building an extensive direct sales and service organization of its own.

Ainsworth has said the company plans to continue expanding its product line to address additional equipment types beyond its initial guidance and monitoring attachments, with ongoing input from the wheat producers who make up its primary customer base guiding which features receive development priority.
