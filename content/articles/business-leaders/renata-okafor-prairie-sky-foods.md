Prairie Sky Foods, a food processing company based in Broken Arrow, has expanded significantly under founder Renata Okafor, growing from a small specialty foods operation into a regional producer supplying grocery retailers and restaurant distributors across northeastern Oklahoma. The company's growth reflects both Okafor's background in food science and a broader regional trend toward more locally sourced processed food products.

Okafor started the company after working as a food scientist for a larger national food manufacturer, where she developed an interest in building products around Oklahoma-grown ingredients rather than the standardized national supply chains her previous employer relied upon. She has said the company's early years involved considerable trial and error in finding the right balance between ingredient sourcing costs and product pricing that retailers would accept.

## Building Relationships With Local Growers

A defining feature of Prairie Sky Foods' business model has been its direct sourcing relationships with regional farmers, an approach that differs from the more anonymized commodity purchasing common among larger food processors. Okafor has said maintaining these direct relationships requires more logistical coordination than buying through standard commodity markets, but produces a level of ingredient quality and traceability that has become a selling point with retail buyers increasingly interested in regional sourcing stories.

The company has expanded its supplier network gradually as production volume has grown, working to maintain the same close working relationships with new growers that characterized its earliest supplier partnerships.

## Scaling Production Without Losing Identity

As demand for its products has grown, Prairie Sky Foods has invested in expanded processing facility capacity, a transition Okafor has described as one of the more difficult periods in the company's growth given the capital investment required and the operational complexity of scaling up food production while maintaining consistent quality and safety standards.

Okafor has emphasized maintaining the company's original identity as a regionally rooted food producer even as it has grown beyond its original specialty foods niche, resisting pressure to pursue rapid national expansion that she has said could dilute the direct grower relationships that distinguish the company from larger competitors.

Looking forward, Okafor has said the company intends to continue growing its regional retail and restaurant distribution footprint while maintaining its commitment to Oklahoma-sourced ingredients as a core part of its business identity.
