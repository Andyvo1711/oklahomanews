Plains Energy Services, a maintenance and field support contractor founded by Felix Noor near Woodward, has grown into one of the region's more established service providers for both wind energy operators and oil field equipment maintenance, a dual focus that Noor has said reflects the diversified nature of western Oklahoma's energy economy rather than a bet on any single energy source.

Noor started the company after years working as a field technician for larger national service providers, where he noticed that smaller regional energy operators often struggled to get timely maintenance support from national contractors whose scheduling priorities favored their largest clients. He has said he built the company specifically to serve that underserved segment of smaller and mid-sized operators.

## Serving Two Energy Sectors at Once

Unlike many maintenance contractors that specialize exclusively in either wind or conventional oil and gas equipment, Plains Energy Services has maintained technical capacity across both sectors, an approach Noor has said provides more stable year-round work for the company's technicians compared to firms dependent on a single energy source's maintenance cycles.

This dual-sector approach required building and maintaining a broader base of technical certifications and equipment knowledge than a single-focus competitor would need, an investment Noor has described as worthwhile given the diversification benefits it provides against downturns that might affect one energy sector more than another at any given time.

## Retaining Technicians in a Competitive Market

Skilled field technicians remain in high demand across western Oklahoma's energy sector, and Plains Energy Services has faced ongoing competition for qualified staff from both energy operators hiring maintenance staff directly and other service contractors. Noor has said the company has worked to differentiate itself as an employer through more predictable scheduling and dedicated regional routes, reducing the extensive travel that some field service jobs require.

The company has also invested in ongoing technical training for its staff, ensuring technicians remain current on the specific equipment used by its various clients, an investment Noor considers necessary given how quickly certain equipment technology has evolved across both the wind and conventional energy sectors.

Noor has said Plains Energy Services plans to continue expanding its service territory gradually across western Oklahoma, guided by demand from existing clients rather than an aggressive push into new markets that might strain the company's technical staffing capacity.
