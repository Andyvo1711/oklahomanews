Marcus Ellery leased his first truck fourteen years ago to haul freight between Tulsa-area warehouses on short-term contracts, work he took on while still holding a full-time job elsewhere. Today his company, Redbud Logistics, operates a fleet of trucks serving distribution centers across the Tulsa metro, part of a freight and logistics sector that has grown alongside the region's expanding warehouse and distribution footprint.

Ellery has said the company's early growth came almost entirely from word of mouth among warehouse managers who needed reliable short-haul freight service and were frustrated by larger national carriers that treated smaller regional contracts as an afterthought. Redbud built its reputation on responsiveness, taking on loads at short notice that bigger carriers were unwilling to prioritize.

## Filling a Gap Left by National Carriers

Large national freight companies typically optimize their networks around long-haul routes and major distribution hubs, which can leave regional, short-haul freight needs underserved, particularly for smaller warehouse operators who don't generate enough volume to command priority service from a national carrier. Ellery has described Redbud's niche as essentially the opposite: a company built specifically to handle the kind of shorter, less predictable freight runs that larger carriers consider low priority.

That focus has meant Redbud's growth has tracked closely with the broader expansion of warehouse and distribution space around Tulsa, as more companies have opened regional distribution centers in the metro area to serve customers across the surrounding region. Ellery has said each new warehouse opening in the area tends to generate inquiries from operators looking for a reliable local freight partner rather than relying solely on national carriers.

## Managing Growth Without Losing Reliability

As Redbud has grown from a single truck to a full fleet, Ellery has said the biggest operational challenge has been maintaining the same responsiveness that built the company's reputation in the first place, even as dispatch decisions have grown more complex with more trucks and more customers to coordinate. The company has invested in dispatch software to help manage routing efficiently, but Ellery maintains that direct relationships between dispatchers and warehouse managers remain the core of how Redbud operates, rather than treating logistics purely as a scheduling problem to be automated.

Looking ahead, Ellery has said he expects continued growth in Tulsa's warehouse and distribution sector to keep generating demand for the kind of flexible, regionally focused freight service Redbud specializes in, though he has also acknowledged that fuel costs and driver recruitment remain ongoing challenges for a company of Redbud's size.
