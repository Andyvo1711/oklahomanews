Bellweather Construction Partners has grown into one of Norman's more established residential and light commercial construction firms under founder Tom Bellweather, who started the company as a small custom home-building operation before expanding into larger multi-unit residential and small commercial projects as demand in the area grew.

Bellweather, who worked as a project superintendent for a larger regional builder before starting his own company, has said he founded the firm with a focus on maintaining closer client relationships than he felt was possible working within a larger organization managing many simultaneous projects with limited individual attention.

## Scaling While Staying Hands-On

As the company has grown, Bellweather has maintained a relatively flat management structure, with project managers reporting directly to him rather than through multiple layers of middle management common at larger construction firms. He has said this structure allows him to stay closely involved in major project decisions even as the company has taken on larger and more complex work.

The shift from single-family custom homes to multi-unit residential and commercial projects required the company to build new expertise in areas like commercial code compliance and larger-scale project financing coordination, a transition Bellweather has described as gradual rather than an abrupt strategic pivot.

## Navigating Labor and Material Costs

Like many construction firms in the region, Bellweather Construction Partners has faced ongoing challenges related to skilled labor availability and fluctuating material costs, pressures that have required careful project bidding and scheduling to maintain profitability. Bellweather has said the company has responded by building longer-term relationships with a core group of subcontractors rather than bidding out every project to the lowest-cost available crew, a strategy intended to ensure more consistent quality and availability even during periods of high regional construction demand.

The company has also invested in training programs for entry-level construction workers, partnering with local career and technology centers to build a pipeline of workers familiar with the company's specific quality standards and safety practices before they join full-time crews.

Bellweather has said the company plans to continue growing its commercial project portfolio while maintaining its original residential construction business, viewing the two segments as complementary rather than competing priorities as Norman's building market continues to evolve.
