Wade Pruitt opened a small leather goods and boot repair storefront in downtown Ardmore fifteen years ago, working mostly on custom repairs and small production runs for local customers. Today his company, Red River Boot & Leather, wholesales its products to retailers across southern Oklahoma and into north Texas, a growth path Pruitt has attributed to steadily building a reputation for quality before ever attempting to scale production.

Pruitt, who trained under an older leatherworker before opening his own shop, has said the early years of the business were dominated by repair work and small custom orders, income that was modest but steady enough to fund gradual investment in better equipment and additional staff over time.

## Building Wholesale Relationships Slowly

The shift from a retail storefront to a wholesale supplier didn't happen through a single large contract, according to Pruitt, but through years of smaller retailers in the region discovering the shop's products and placing modest initial orders that grew over time as trust in the product's quality and consistency built up. He has said this slow-growth approach meant Red River Boot & Leather avoided some of the cash flow problems that can sink small manufacturers who try to scale production too quickly to meet an early, unproven wholesale demand.

Southern Oklahoma and north Texas share a strong regional market for western wear and leather goods, and Pruitt has said proximity to that customer base, along with the ability to visit retail partners in person rather than managing relationships purely by phone or email, has been a meaningful advantage over competitors based farther away.

## Managing Growth While Preserving Craft

As wholesale orders have grown, Pruitt has added staff trained in the company's specific production techniques, though he has said maintaining consistent quality across a larger team required more structured training than the informal apprenticeship model that worked when the shop was smaller. Red River Boot & Leather has also invested in updated equipment to handle increased production volume without sacrificing the hand-finished details that Pruitt says distinguish the company's products from mass-produced alternatives.

Looking ahead, Pruitt has said he is cautious about growing wholesale distribution too far beyond the regional footprint the company currently serves, preferring to deepen relationships with existing retail partners in southern Oklahoma and north Texas rather than pursuing broader national distribution that he believes would require compromises in how the products are made.
