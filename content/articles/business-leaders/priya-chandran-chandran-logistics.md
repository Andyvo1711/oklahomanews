Chandran Logistics Group, a freight and warehousing company based in Tulsa, has expanded steadily under founder Priya Chandran, growing from a small trucking operation into a mid-sized logistics provider serving manufacturers across northeastern Oklahoma. The company's growth has tracked closely with the broader expansion of manufacturing activity in the Tulsa area, as more companies have sought reliable regional freight partners rather than relying solely on national carriers.

Chandran started the company after working for several years in operations management at a larger logistics firm, where she identified what she has described as a gap in service for mid-sized manufacturers whose freight volumes were too large for small local carriers but too specialized for national companies focused on standardized routes.

## Filling a Middle Market Niche

The company built its early client base by focusing specifically on manufacturers needing flexible, relationship-driven freight arrangements rather than the standardized service offered by larger national carriers. Chandran has said this middle-market focus allowed the company to differentiate itself without competing directly against much larger, better-capitalized logistics companies.

As the client base grew, Chandran Logistics Group added warehousing capacity to its service offerings, allowing manufacturing clients to store inventory closer to their production facilities rather than relying entirely on just-in-time delivery from distant suppliers. This expansion into warehousing has become an increasingly important part of the company's revenue, reflecting broader shifts in how manufacturers manage supply chain risk.

## Growing a Regional Workforce

The company's growth has required steady expansion of its driver and warehouse staff, a challenge Chandran has said requires ongoing attention given competitive pressure from other logistics employers in the region. The company has emphasized retention through consistent scheduling and route assignments, an approach intended to differentiate it from logistics employers known for unpredictable schedules that make it difficult for drivers to plan their personal lives.

Chandran has also prioritized hiring drivers with strong local route knowledge, given the company's focus on the northeastern Oklahoma region rather than long-haul national routes, allowing for more predictable home time compared to over-the-road trucking positions.

Looking ahead, Chandran has said the company intends to continue expanding warehousing capacity to meet growing manufacturer demand, while maintaining the relationship-focused service model that has defined its growth since the company's founding.
