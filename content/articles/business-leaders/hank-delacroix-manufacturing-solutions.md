Delacroix Manufacturing Solutions, a metal fabrication and industrial parts company based in Ardmore, has undergone a significant modernization effort under the leadership of Hank Delacroix, who took over the family-founded business from his father a decade ago and has since worked to update aging equipment while preserving the specialized production knowledge built up over the company's earlier decades of operation.

The company, originally founded as a general metal fabrication shop, has narrowed its focus over the years toward specialized industrial parts production, a shift Delacroix has said reflects both changing market demand and the company's own growing expertise in more technically demanding fabrication work.

## Balancing Modernization With Institutional Knowledge

Updating the company's production equipment presented a particular challenge, since much of the specialized knowledge required to produce certain parts had developed informally over years of experience among the company's longest-tenured machinists, knowledge that was not always fully documented in formal procedures. Delacroix has said a significant part of the modernization effort involved working with veteran staff to document their processes before introducing new computer-controlled equipment intended to complement, rather than replace, that expertise.

This approach required more time than a straightforward equipment replacement might have, but Delacroix has said it was necessary to avoid losing production quality during the transition, given how much of the company's reputation rested on the precision and reliability of parts that depended on accumulated shop-floor experience.

## Investing in the Next Generation of Workers

As part of the modernization effort, the company has also expanded its apprenticeship program, pairing younger workers with veteran machinists nearing retirement to ensure institutional knowledge transfers before those experienced workers leave the workforce. Delacroix has described this succession planning as one of his top priorities since taking over company leadership, given the company's aging workforce at the time of the transition.

The company has partnered with a regional career and technology center to identify apprenticeship candidates, an arrangement that has helped build a more predictable pipeline of new talent than the company's previous, largely informal hiring approach.

Delacroix has said the company plans to continue its modernization investments gradually, prioritizing equipment upgrades that support its specialized fabrication niche while maintaining the mentorship-driven approach to workforce development that he considers central to preserving the company's manufacturing reputation in the region.
