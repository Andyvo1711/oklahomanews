Marsh Hospitality Group has opened a series of boutique lodging properties across northern Oklahoma under founder Lucia Marsh, targeting a segment of the travel market that Marsh has said was underserved by the standardized chain hotels that dominate lodging options in smaller Oklahoma cities. The company's properties emphasize distinct local design elements and smaller room counts than typical chain hotel developments.

Marsh entered the hospitality industry after a career in commercial real estate development, where she grew familiar with property renovation and repositioning projects. She has said she saw an opportunity in northern Oklahoma's smaller cities, where business travelers and visitors often had limited lodging options beyond standard highway chain hotels lacking much local character.

## Renovating Rather Than Building New

Rather than pursuing ground-up new construction, Marsh Hospitality Group has focused primarily on renovating existing historic buildings, converting former commercial properties into boutique lodging with design elements reflecting local architectural history. Marsh has said this approach requires more complex renovation planning than new construction but produces properties with a distinct character that helps differentiate them from standardized chain competitors.

The renovation-focused strategy has also meant navigating the particular challenges of working with older building structures, including unexpected issues that often arise once renovation work begins on decades-old buildings. Marsh has described these challenges as a routine, if sometimes costly, part of the company's business model.

## Serving Business and Leisure Travelers Alike

Marsh Hospitality Group's properties have been designed to appeal to both business travelers visiting the region's energy and manufacturing employers and leisure travelers drawn to northern Oklahoma's smaller-town character. Marsh has said balancing these two customer segments has required careful attention to amenities, ensuring properties offer reliable business traveler necessities like strong internet connectivity while maintaining the aesthetic appeal that draws leisure guests.

The company has worked to build relationships with local businesses and event organizers, positioning its properties as preferred lodging partners for corporate visitors and small conferences that might otherwise default to standard chain hotel bookings.

Marsh has said the company plans to continue evaluating additional renovation opportunities across northern Oklahoma's smaller communities, with a focus on buildings that offer both architectural character and practical suitability for conversion into boutique lodging.
