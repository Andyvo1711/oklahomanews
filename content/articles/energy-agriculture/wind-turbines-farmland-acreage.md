A Sept. 3 commentary in Oklahoma Business Voice argued that wind turbines take up little farmland and that renewables can offer stability for Oklahoma growers and rural communities.

## The Argument

The piece cites an estimate of about one to two acres out of production per turbine and says producers can farm around a turbine or run cattle nearby.

## Context

Oklahoma wind projects continue to advance. Invenergy's proposed States Edge project in the Panhandle would span Cimarron and Texas counties, and its Overland Station project near McAlester would use 35 turbines.

## Rural Debate

Landowners in some regions have opposed new energy infrastructure, as in northeast Oklahoma, where AEP's proposed 345 kV line drew protests. Supporters of renewables point to lease payments and local tax revenue.
