Oklahoma producers are weighing volatile markets and dry soil as the winter wheat planting window opens, Oklahoma State University crop marketing specialist Todd Hubbs said in a Sept. 24 Oklahoma Farm Report interview.

## Wheat

Hard red winter wheat contracts fell about 30 cents from Monday's highs. Hubbs said "the core fundamentals for wheat have not undergone a dramatic shift," and the main problem is getting money back into commodities. Many growers are hesitant to plant into dry ground, and central and northeastern Oklahoma need significant rain. A Super El Niño pattern could bring wetter winters to the Southern Plains.

## Exports and Soybeans

U.S. wheat sales are running well behind their historical pace, and high U.S. prices are dampening foreign demand. New-crop soybean contracts are above $13 per bushel, which Hubbs called a respectable pricing opportunity for producers needing cash flow.

## Global Factors

Russia declared an emergency in key wheat regions because of logistical backlogs, and Danube River vessel waits are two to three times longer than normal.
