Oklahoma is in its most extensive and severe drought since 2012, and the dry soil is holding back winter wheat planting, DTN Progressive Farmer reported Sept. 16.

## The Drought by the Numbers

As of the U.S. Drought Monitor for Sept. 8, 100% of Oklahoma was in dryness or drought, and almost 89% was in severe, extreme or exceptional drought. A year earlier, 25% of the state had some dryness or drought and none was in severe drought or worse.

Central and western Oklahoma had more than 50 days at or above 100 degrees this year, according to the Oklahoma Mesonet; the southwestern corner had 70. Central and western areas received no more than 4 inches of rain from June through August, mostly 25% to 75% of normal.

## Wheat and Cattle

USDA data for the week ending Sept. 13 showed Oklahoma wheat seeding at 3% complete, half the five-year average of 7%, largely because of insufficient soil moisture for germination. At least a month of the growing window has been lost, delaying wheat pasture that Oklahoma's beef industry relies on.

## Outlook

Forecasts through the end of September pointed to moderate to heavy rain across the Southern Plains, which could improve seeding conditions.
