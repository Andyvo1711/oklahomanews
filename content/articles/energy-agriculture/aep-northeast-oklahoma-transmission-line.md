A 345-kilovolt transmission line proposed by AEP Oklahoma Transmission Company is drawing opposition from landowners and tribes in northeastern Oklahoma, Oklahoma Energy Today reported Sept. 25.

## The Project

The 316-mile line, mandated by the Southwest Power Pool, would run from Buffalo Flats Substation near Garden Plain, Kansas, to Delaware Substation in Oklahoma, as part of the Branson 345 kV Overlay Project. The Oklahoma portion is split into east and west cases, with the west segment about 34 miles through Washington and Nowata counties. Construction would begin in summer 2028 and finish in late 2029.

## Opposition

As of Aug. 4, the Corporation Commission had received 203 notices of protest and 34 pro se motions. Landowners say they have not been allowed to participate meaningfully. Tribes including the Eastern Shawnee, Modoc, Ottawa, Peoria and Quapaw cited threats to cultural practices and preservation, bird collisions and noise. Natalie Bullard of Protect Rural Oklahoma said, "We do not want AEP running over anybody."

## Rulings

An administrative law judge treated most protests as public comment but allowed five landowners and Protect Rural Oklahoma to intervene. AEP's Jeffrey Ellis argued delay would raise risks of overloads and load shedding during winter storms. The Attorney General's office did not object to the certificate request.
