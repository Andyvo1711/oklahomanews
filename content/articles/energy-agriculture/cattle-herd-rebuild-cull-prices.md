Limited cattle supplies are keeping livestock prices at historic highs, but analysts say a herd rebuild will be slow, according to an Oklahoma Farm Report article Oct. 14.

## Rain Brings Relief

Recent widespread Plains rainfall has improved outlooks. Oklahoma Mesonet data show 1 to 3 inches of rain in southern and central parts of the state, while northwestern regions still have drier subsoil.

## Cull Prices Drop

Cull cow prices have fallen significantly in recent weeks, which gives producers less reason to sell older cows and may keep them for another calf crop. Tim Struck of AgMarket.Net projects at least two years before the domestic beef herd sees meaningful, structural expansion, noting, "It's not as attractive to cull cows."

## A Slow Rebuild

Oklahoma State University Extension livestock marketing specialist Derrell Peel said price signals encourage rebuilding but operations move slowly. Prolonged drought forced deeper liquidation, so the herd starts from historic multi-decade lows, and heifer retention has not been aggressive because pasture recovery takes time. Calf values remain strong and stocker demand is high, so many producers are using that cash flow rather than retaining replacement heifers.
